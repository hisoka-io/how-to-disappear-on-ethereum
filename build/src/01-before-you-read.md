\chapter*{Prelude}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Prelude}
\thispagestyle{empty}

Ethereum makes it possible to move money, build applications, and
verify transactions without relying on a central authority. But using
it also leaves traces, some obvious, others surprisingly difficult to
notice.

This book is about understanding those traces.

It explores what becomes visible when you use Ethereum, from
transactions recorded on the blockchain to the information your wallet
shares with servers, applications, and the wider network.

It also looks at the technologies being built to protect that
information. Some hide transaction details. Others protect your network
activity or make it harder to connect one action to another. Each
solves a different part of the problem, and each comes with
limitations.

You don't need to know cryptography to follow along. We'll introduce
technical ideas when they become useful, explain how the approaches work,
and look at what they protect, what they leave exposed, and where trust
still matters.

This is not a guide to achieving perfect anonymity, nor a
recommendation to use any particular protocol. Privacy is rarely a
single setting or a single tool. It is a series of decisions, often
involving information you didn't realise you were sharing.

You can read from beginning to end or start wherever your curiosity
takes you. Sources are included throughout for anyone who wants to
explore further.

This is the first edition of this book, prepared for Devcon 2026. Some
of the technologies discussed here will change, and new approaches
will emerge.

The details will evolve. The questions are worth continuing to ask.
